To Toronto

By Alberto Del Valle

Est. read time: 3 mins.

I just moved to Toronto – and for those of you wondering, it’s actually my second time doing so. I first arrived in August 2023, but after living here for over two years, I had to make the difficult decision to move back to my original country. Truthfully, it wasn’t much of a decision: my visa had expired, and staying in Canada without legal status was never an option for me.

Fast forward to 2026: IRCC office invited me to apply for Permanent Residency. After spending the better part of the year gathering documentation and submitting my application, I was officiatly approved in August and I was given the green light to return. Hurray! (I'm skipping a lot of detail here – it was an arduous six-month process – but if you’re curious about any specific step, send me your comments or questions and I’ll cover them in a future post. You can reach me on X or email me.)



Why Toronto?

I’ve lost count of how many times I’ve been asked this question, but I have two very well-rehearsed answers.

Long Story Short:

I initially set my sights on the U.S., while my wife had her heart set on B.C. After a lot of deliberation, Toronto became our middle ground – the one place where we both had the best shot at advancing our careers.

The Longer Story:

I grew up in a small industrial city with fair opportunities and not much to do. By the time I graduated university, I knew I wanted to live in a major urban center, but I had to bide my time.

Early on in our relationship, my wife – girlfriend at the time – and I both realized we shared the dream of living abroad. We had each visited Toronto on separate occasions, and after seeing what Canadian cities had to offer, we were almost certain where we were headed.

Amost.

I think it’s easier to fall for American cities than for any others in North America. For me, movies did much of the heavy lifting. Growing up, watching those cities come alive on film, you can’t help but wonder what they look like in person. When you get to see those landmarks for yourself, you either like them even more or find them dull and unimpressive. Their popularity precedes their reputation. I’ve visited a lot of these big cities — New York, Boston, D.C., Houston, L.A., Las Vegas, among others — and each visit reinforced the feeling that I wanted to move to a metropolis: so much life and opportunity around every corner. You can’t blame me for wanting to move to the U.S.

Over time, as America’s international reputation decayed, so did my desire to relocate there. Year by year, as we watched our savings grow, we knew we needed to have a serious conversation about where to go. And talk seriously we did. After spending weeks thinking it over and considering our options, we were finally down to two countries: Canada and New Zealand. As attractive as moving to Aotearoa sounded, Canada ended up being the best option for two reasons: money and proximity. Traveling to the other side of the world was expensive, and our money would take us further if we stayed in North America. Less travel time meant cheaper airfares, which meant more room to pack more stuff as well.”

So yeah, all those years ago, when we finally had enough savings to move abroad, we packed up our things, held each other tight, and took the biggest leap of faith of our lives. No jobs or friends, just a couple with a dream.
