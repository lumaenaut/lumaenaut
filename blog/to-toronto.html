To Toronto

By Alberto Del Valle

Est. read time: 3 mins.

I just moved to Toronto—and for those of you wondering, it’s actually my second time doing so. I first arrived in August 2023, but after living here for over two years, I had to make the difficult decision to move back to my original country. Truthfully, it wasn’t much of a decision: my visa had expired, and staying in Canada without legal status was never an option for me.

Fast forward to 2026: IRCC office invited me to apply for Permanent Residency. After spending the better part of the year gathering documentation and submitting my application, I was officiatly approved in August and I was given the green light to return. Hurray! (I'm skipping a lot of detail here—it was an arduous six-month process—but if you’re curious about any specific step, send me your comments or questions and I’ll cover them in a future post. You can reach me on X or email me.)



Why Toronto?

I’ve lost count of how many times I’ve been asked this question, but I have two very well-rehearsed answers.

Long Story Short:

I initially set my sights on the U.S., while my wife had her heart set on B.C. After a lot of deliberation, Toronto became our middle ground—the one place we thought both had the best shot at advancing our careers.

The Long Answer:

I grew up in a small industrial city with fair opportunities and not much to do. By the time I graduated university, I knew I wanted to live in a major urban center, but I had to bide my time.

Early on in our relationship, my wife—girlfriend at the time—and I both realized we shared the dream of living abroad. We had each visited Toronto on separate occasions, and after seeing what Canadian cities had to offer, we were almost certain where we were headed.

Amost.

It’s easy to fall for American cities. Growing up watching them come alive on film, you can’t help but wonder "what do they look like in person?". When you finally stand in front of those iconic landmarks, you either love them even more or find them surprisingly dull—their popularity always precedes them. Having visited places like New York, Boston, D.C., Houston, Las Vegas, and L.A., every trip reinforced my desire to settle in a bustling metropolis full of energy and opportunity. You can’t blame me for wanting to call the U.S. home.

Overtimer, however, as the political climate shifted, so did my desire to relocate there…

Year by year, as our savings grew, we knew it was time for a serious conversation about our future overseas. After weeks of evaluating options, we narrowed it down to two destinations: Canada and New Zealand. As tempting as Aotearoa sounded, Canada ultimately won out for two very practical reasons: cost and proximity. Flying across the globe was expensive, and staying in North America meant our money went much further; shorter travel times meant cheaper airfare—and more budget left over to pack up our lives.

So, all those years ago, when we finally saved enough to take the plunge, we packed our bags, held each other tight, and took the biggest leap of faith of our lives. No jobs, no safety net, no local friends—just a couple with a dream.
