To Toronto

By Alberto Del Valle

Est. read time: 3 mins.

I just moved to Toronto, and for those of you wondering, it’s actually my second time doing so. I first moved here in August 2023, and after living here for more than two years, I had to make the painful decision to move back to my original country. It wasn’t really a decision: my visa had expired, and staying in Canada without status just wasn’t in the cards for me.

Early this year — 2026 — the IRCC office invited me to apply for permanent residence. After spending the better part of the year working on my application and submitting all the required documentation, my application was approved in August and I was given the green light to move back. Hurray! (I’ve skipped a lot of details; it was a six-month process overall. If you’re interested in any particular part, send me your questions and I’ll happily answer them in a future post. You can reach me on X or by email.)

Why Toronto?

Honestly, I’ve lost count of how many times I’ve been asked this question, so I have a very well-rehearsed answer.

Short answer

“I initially wanted to move to the States; my wife wanted to move to B.C. But after giving it a lot of thought, we decided Toronto would be the best place for both of us to find the best opportunities to further our careers.”

Toronto — for some reason — became the middle ground for what we both wanted.

The long answer

“I grew up in a small industrial city. There wasn’t much to do or many places to visit. By the time I graduated from university, I knew I wanted to move to a big urban centre. I had to bide my time.

My wife (my girlfriend at the time) and I visited Toronto on separate occasions. We had just started dating, but early on we already knew we wanted to live abroad. After seeing what Canadian cities had to offer, there was almost no doubt about where we were headed… almost.

I think it’s easier to fall for American cities than for any others in North America. For me, movies did much of the heavy lifting. Growing up, watching those cities come alive on film, you can’t help but wonder what they look like in person. When you get to see those landmarks for yourself, you either like them even more or find them dull and unimpressive. Their popularity precedes their reputation. I’ve visited a lot of these big cities — New York, Boston, D.C., Houston, L.A., Las Vegas, among others — and each visit reinforced the feeling that I wanted to move to a metropolis: so much life and opportunity around every corner. You can’t blame me for wanting to move to the U.S.

Over time, as America’s international reputation decayed, so did my desire to relocate there. Year by year, as we watched our savings grow, we knew we needed to have a serious conversation about where to go. And talk seriously we did. After spending weeks thinking it over and considering our options, we were finally down to two countries: Canada and New Zealand. As attractive as moving to Aotearoa sounded, Canada ended up being the best option for two reasons: money and proximity. Traveling to the other side of the world was expensive, and our money would take us further if we stayed in North America. Less travel time meant cheaper airfares, which meant more room to pack more stuff as well.”

So yeah, all those years ago, when we finally had enough savings to move abroad, we packed up our things, held each other tight, and took the biggest leap of faith of our lives. No jobs or friends, just a couple with a dream.
